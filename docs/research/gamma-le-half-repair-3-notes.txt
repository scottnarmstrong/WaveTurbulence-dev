1.2 What exactly the proof uses (the "structural fact")

  Let V be a vine with joints v1, v2 and groups G (a partition of the non-joint atoms).
  (U0) [L1519]  V consists of at most 3 ladders plus O(1) further atoms (TARGET says < 20).  If V has 2m+5 atoms,
       there are exactly m+1 groups.
  (U1) [L1523, spine]  There are groups G_1, ..., G_r (r >= 0) such that:
       (a) for every i, in V - G_i the set {v2} u G_{<i} lies in one component and {v1} u G_{>i} in another.
           This is what makes every path from v2 to v1 meet G_1, ..., G_r in this order.
       (b) consecutive members of (v2, G_1, ..., G_r, v1) are joined by a bond.  The product bound also uses that
           non-consecutive members are not joined; this follows from (a).
  (U2) [L1523, L1525-L1531, pendant]  The atoms in no G_i and not joints form a set H with:
       either H is empty,
       or H = Y_1 u ... u Y_s (groups), and for every k the set H_k := Y_k u ... u Y_s
         * consists of atoms of degree 4,
         * is joined to V - H_k by exactly two bonds,
         * those bonds are single bonds x-y and x'-y' with y, y' in Y_k ("v2, v4 in the same group").
       The induction "remove (v2,v4) and apply the same argument to H'" is exactly the peeling Y_1, Y_2, ...
       The two outer ends x, x' of H must have layers in [q', q].  This holds when they lie in spine groups or are
       joints.
  For a (V)-type vine, r = 0 and H = all non-joint atoms.

1.3 Theorem 6 (PROVED for every T-vine; CHECKED, Sec. 1.6)

  Setting.  B is a T-vine (N2 Def 1.2 / Def 5.1) with joints j, j'; either labelling (v1, v2) = (j, j') or (j', j).
  Pieces are the classes of atoms of B not separated by any 2-edge-cut.  The groups of B are its non-joint
  pieces.  By N2 Prop 1.17(b), the pieces are: {j}, {j'}, one 3-atom core of shape (B), (C) or (D), and m 2-atom
  pieces, each joined internally by 1 or 2 bonds, where |B| = 2m+5.  So B has m+1 groups.
  Gamma = Gamma(B) is the multigraph with one node per piece and one edge per bond of B between distinct pieces.

  Theorem 6.  For every T-vine B and either labelling:
   (i)   Gamma is a cactus (every edge lies on exactly one cycle).  All its cycles have length 2, except at most
         one of length 3.
   (ii)  [spine] Let G_1, ..., G_r be the non-joint pieces whose removal separates v1 from v2, ordered by the
         nested v2-sides.  Then (U1)(a) holds (atom level).  Consecutive members of
         (v2, G_1, ..., G_r, v1) are joined by exactly 2 bonds, except at most one consecutive pair joined by 1 bond.
         Non-consecutive members are not joined.  r = 0 iff sigma(B) = 1.
   (iii) [pendant] Let H := the non-joint atoms not in any G_i.  Then H is empty iff the core has shape (B) or (C).
         If the core has shape (D), H = Y_1 u ... u Y_s (s >= 1), where:
           * Y_1, ..., Y_{s-1} are rungs: 2-atom pieces with an internal double bond, each atom having one bond
             toward Y_{k-1} (or the outside) and one toward Y_{k+1};
           * Y_s is the (D) core;
           * Y_k and Y_{k+1} are joined by exactly two bonds;
           * every H_k = Y_k u ... u Y_s is joined to B - H_k by exactly two single bonds x-y, x'-y' with
             y != y' in Y_k and x != x'.
         So H is "Vine (V) minus its two joints" with s-1 rungs (s-1 >= 0).  H is attached in one of two ways:
           (P-a) both outer ends x, x' lie in one spine piece G_i.  This G_i is the unique piece whose Gamma-degree
                 is 6: a 2-atom piece with a single internal bond (the (VII) pair of `cohmol`);
           (P-b) x and x' lie in two consecutive members of (v2, G_1, ..., G_r, v1).  These are joined by a single
                 bond, and the three nodes form the unique cycle of length 3 of Gamma.
   (iv)  [shape constants, (U0)] B has at most 3 maximal ladders and at most 13 atoms outside them, and m+1 groups.

  Consequently (U0)-(U2) hold for every T-vine, and TARGET's proof of Prop `layervine` (L1519-L1531) applies
  verbatim to the gamma <= 1/2 family, with groups := pieces.  Monotone layering along the spine gives
  C_0^{m+|q-q'|}; the pendant is in one layer between the layers of x and x', which lie in [q', q].

1.4 Proofs

  Lemma 6.1 (cactus).  PROVED.
    Let B be a T-vine and Gamma its piece quotient.  Then Gamma is a connected loopless multigraph.  Every node
    has even degree.  Any two distinct nodes are separated by a cut of exactly 2 edges.  Hence every block
    (maximal 2-connected subgraph) of Gamma is a cycle of length >= 2, and Gamma is a cactus.
    Proof.
      - Gamma is connected because B is.  It is loopless by construction.
      - deg_Gamma(P) = |delta_B(P)| = sum_{v in P} deg_B(v) - 2 e_B(P), which is even because every B-degree is
        2 or 4.  So every cut of Gamma is even, and Gamma has no bridge.
      - By the definition of pieces, two distinct pieces P, P' are separated by a 2-edge-cut delta_B(X) of B.
        X is a union of pieces (a 2-edge-cut never splits a piece), so delta_B(X) is a 2-edge cut of Gamma
        separating P and P'.  Hence lambda_Gamma(P, P') = 2.
      - Let beta be a block with at least 2 edges (a 1-edge block would be a bridge).
          * If beta has 2 nodes, it is a bundle of k >= 2 parallel edges, and k >= 3 would give lambda >= 3.
            So beta is a digon.
          * If beta has >= 3 nodes and is not a cycle, then, being 2-connected, it contains a cycle C and a path
            P' (possibly a single parallel edge) with distinct ends x, y on C and no inner node on C (an ear).
            Then the two arcs of C and P' are 3 edge-disjoint x-y paths, so lambda(x, y) >= 3, a contradiction.
      - Every edge lies in exactly one block, hence on exactly one cycle.  QED
    Standard facts used below:
      - a node of Gamma-degree 2k lies on exactly k cycles, so degree-2 nodes are not cut nodes;
      - two edges on a common cycle form a 2-edge-cut of Gamma, hence of B (same bonds);
      - for nodes u, w that each lie on one cycle, the block-cut tree contains a unique path
        C_1, A_1, C_2, ..., A_{t-1}, C_t with u on C_1 and w on C_t; the nodes separating u from w are exactly
        A_1, ..., A_{t-1}.

  Lemma 6.2 (no two pendants; N2 Lemma 1.6 re-proved for an arbitrary (Y2)-path).  PROVED.
    A T-vine B has no two disjoint nonempty sets K_1, K_2 of interior atoms with
    |delta_B(K_1)| = |delta_B(K_2)| = 2.
    Proof.
    Step 1 (transfer to the closure).  Let G = cl(B), a connected loopless 4-regular multigraph on the interior
    atoms.  Suppose K_1, K_2 are as stated.
      - B - K_i is connected.  Each component Y of B - K_i has delta(Y) contained in delta(K_i), and |delta(Y)|
        is even and >= 2.  So there is only one component.
      - Hence neither joint has both of its B-neighbours in K_i (sigma = 0), and the joint path n1-j-j'-n2
        (sigma = 1) does not have both ends in K_i.  Otherwise B - K_i would consist of joints only, so
        K_i = I(B), contradicting K_1 n K_2 = {} with both nonempty.
      - Therefore every bond of delta_B(K_i) corresponds to exactly one bond of delta_G(K_i): itself, or the
        contracted bond through the joint(s).  So |delta_G(K_i)| = 2.
      - No contracted bond lies inside K_i, so the bonds of G inside K_i are bonds of B.  Hence K_i has no
        internal triple bond.
    Step 2 (along the reduction).  By the definition of a T-vine there are (Y2) steps
    G = G_0 -> G_1 -> ... -> G_N ~= T.  A (Y2) step keeps a graph connected, loopless and 4-regular: a path
    through the removed pair {a, b} enters at c and leaves at d, and is replaced by the new bond c-d.
      - Let t_i be the first step that deletes an atom of K_i or adds a bond with both ends in K_i (t_i = infinity
        if none).  For m < t_i, K_i is intact in G_m: all its atoms are present, its internal bonds are those of
        G, and |delta_{G_m}(K_i)| = 2.
        Induction: let a step at the triple pair {a, b} have external bonds a-c and b-d.  The pair cannot lie
        inside an intact K_i (no internal triple bond) or straddle it (3 crossing bonds).  If exactly one of c, d
        is in K_i, a crossing bond is replaced by a crossing bond.  If both are, the step is step t_i.
      - At step t_i the two crossing bonds of K_i are a-c, b-d.  Then K_i u {a, b} has no bond leaving it, so the
        atoms of G_{t_i - 1} are exactly K_i u {a, b}.
      - Suppose t_1 <= t_2 with t_1 finite.  At time t_1 - 1, K_2 is intact, so K_2 is a subset of the atom set
        K_1 u {a, b}.  Hence K_2 is contained in {a, b}.  An intact singleton would have |delta| = 4, so
        K_2 = {a, b}, which has the internal triple bond a=b.  This is a contradiction.
      - So t_1 = t_2 = infinity, and K_1 is intact in G_N ~= T.  But every nonempty proper atom set of T has
        |delta| = 4, and K_1 = all of T would give |delta| = 0, not 2.  This is a contradiction.  QED
    (No confluence (N2 Lemma 1.4) is needed: only the one (Y2)-path given by the definition is used.)

  Lemma 6.3 (attachment units).  PROVED.
    Fix the labelling and the block-cut path C_1, A_1, ..., A_{t-1}, C_t from v2 to v1 (A_0 := v2,
    A_t := v1).  Put S := {A_0, ..., A_t}.  Define the attachment units:
      (arc)     a maximal arc of C_i strictly between A_{i-1} and A_i that contains at least one node.  C_i has at
                most two arcs.
      (hanging) a cycle D != C_i, C_{i+1} through some A_i with 1 <= i <= t-1.  No cycle other than C_1 (resp.
                C_t) passes through v2 (resp. v1), since these have Gamma-degree 2.
    For each unit, the two edges of its cycle that join it to S form a 2-edge-cut.  Let K(unit) be the side of that
    cut that contains the unit.  Then:
      - K(unit) contains no node of S.  The rest of S stays connected to the unit's S-nodes: through the other arc
        of C_i, or through the chain at A_i.
      - K(unit) is connected, and all its boundary edges go to S, so it is a component of Gamma - S.
      - Every node outside S is reached from S through some unit.
    So the components of Gamma - S are exactly the K(unit).  They are pairwise disjoint and joint-free, and each
    is attached by exactly 2 bonds.  By Lemma 6.2 there is AT MOST ONE UNIT.  QED

  Proof of Theorem 6.
    (i), (ii), cactus part.
      - Spine: the separating pieces are A_1, ..., A_{t-1} (Lemma 6.1).
      - A chain cycle C_i with both arcs empty is a digon.  C_i cannot have both arcs nonempty (two units).  An
        arc with >= 2 nodes Z, Z' is impossible: each of Z, Z' together with everything hanging from it is a
        joint-free 2-pole (cut by the two C_i-edges at that node), and these are disjoint, contradicting
        Lemma 6.2.  Hence every chain cycle is a digon, or a triangle (A_{i-1}, Z, A_i) whose third node Z is
        the top of the unique unit (P-b).
      - Inside the unit, let Y be any node and D' any cycle below Y (hanging from Y away from S).
          * D' has exactly 2 nodes: two nodes of D' - Y would give two disjoint joint-free 2-poles.
          * Y has at most one cycle below it, for the same reason.
        A hanging unit D at A_i is a digon for the same reason.
      - So the unit is a path of digons Y_1 = Y_2 = ... = Y_s.  deg_Gamma(Y_k) = 4 for k < s,
        deg_Gamma(Y_s) = 2, and Y_1 hangs from A_i by a digon (P-a) or is the triangle node Z (P-b).
      - A spine node lies on C_i, C_{i+1} and on at most one hanging digon, so its degree is 4, or 6 iff (P-a).
      - Hence Gamma has at most one cycle of length 3 (P-b only), and all other cycles are digons.  This gives
        the bond counts 2 and 1 in (ii).
      - t = 1 iff v1 and v2 lie on one cycle C_1.  C_1 is then a triangle (v2, Z, v1), because a digon v2 = v1
        would make B = {v1, v2}.  So v1 and v2 are adjacent and sigma = 1.  Conversely, if sigma = 1 the bond
        v1-v2 lies on a cycle through both, so t = 1.  Hence r = t - 1 = 0 iff sigma(B) = 1.
    Translation to atoms (uses the piece-shape theorem, N2 Prop 1.17(b)).
      - Pieces are internally connected (2-atom pieces with >= 1 internal bond; cores (B), (C), (D)).  So
        separation and components in Gamma are the same as in B, which gives (U1)(a).
      - Gamma-degrees: joint 2; (D) core 2; (B)/(C) core 12 - 2*4 = 4; a 2-atom piece 8 - 2*mult, which is 4 or
        6.  Hence:
          * Y_s (degree 2, non-joint) is the (D) core;
          * the Y_k with k < s (degree 4, not the core) are 2-atom pieces with a double bond;
          * a degree-6 spine piece is a 2-atom piece with a single bond.
      - The pendant exists iff the core is (D): a degree-2 non-joint piece cannot be a cut node, so it lies in a
        unit; and a unit ends in a degree-2 piece.
      - One bond each side, and distinct ends: let y in Y_k.
          * If both y-bonds leaving Y_k went into H_{k+1}, then
            |delta(H_{k+1} u {y})| = 2 + 4 - 2*2 = 2.  That 2-cut separates y from its partner, a contradiction.
            The same argument applies to the other side.
          * If both bonds of delta(H_k) ended at one atom y, then |delta(H_k - y)| = 2 - 2 + 2 = 2, again
            separating y from its partner.  Every non-joint piece has >= 2 atoms.
          * If both ended at one outer atom x, then |delta(H u {x})| = 2 + 4 - 4 = 2 separates x from its piece
            partner (x is not a joint: a joint with both bonds into H would give B = H u {x}).
        Two crossing bonds with distinct ends are single bonds.
    (iv) Ladders.
      - Double bonds of B lie inside pieces.  A double bond x=y between different pieces would make the 2-cut of
        its digon separate x from its partner.
      - A ladder step (two disjoint single rails between consecutive double bonds) is a digon between two rung
        pieces.  Its rails are anti-parallel by balance (N1 Lemma D), as B2's `IsLadder` requires.
      - The chain's rung pieces form at most 2 runs.  The only possible break is: the (B)/(C) core; the degree-6
        piece (P-a); or the single bond of the triangle (P-b).  At most one occurs, since a (B)/(C) core excludes a
        pendant.
      - The pendant forms at most 1 run, and only when the core is (D).
      - Each double bond of the core has an endpoint with at most one bond leaving the core: (B) x=y has 1 and 1;
        (C) w1=w2 has w2 with 0; (D) p=r and r=q have r with 0.  So a core double bond is never an interior rung,
        and can at most lengthen one end of a run.
      - Hence there are at most 3 maximal ladders.  The atoms outside them are at most:
          2 (joints) + 3 (core) + 2 (degree-6 piece) + 3 x 2 (runs of length 1) = 13.
      - The group count is m+1 by the piece-shape theorem.  QED

  Remark (REF2's suggested route).
    REF2 proposed: "every cactus cycle has length <= 3, preserved by rung insertion, plus Lemma 1.6".  This also
    works.
      - A rung insertion at a 2-cut {e, f} subdivides e and f and joins the two new atoms by a double bond.  The
        cycle containing e, f (length L) splits into two cycles of lengths k+1 and L-k+1, with 1 <= k <= L-1.
      - So "all lengths <= 3" is preserved, and its base is the D = 0 vines.

4.1 IsTVine (B2 conventions: half-edge molecules, undirected `IsBlock`, `bondsBetween`, `GroupConnected`)

  closure S j1 j2 : Option UMultigraph
    The multigraph on S - {j1, j2}.  Keep the bonds of S not at a joint, then:
      - sigma = 0: add, for each joint, one bond between its two S-neighbours;
      - sigma = 1: add one bond between the S-neighbour of j1 other than j2 and the S-neighbour of j2 other
        than j1.
    The result is none if a loop arises, or if sigma >= 2 (that is vine (I)).
  Y2Step G G'
    a != b are joined by exactly 3 bonds, a has exactly one further bond (to c), b exactly one (to d), c != d,
    and G' = G - {a, b} + {c-d}.
  IsTVine S j1 j2 :=
       IsBlock S j1 j2                                           -- degrees 2/4, connected, atomic group
    /\ (forall u v in S, u != v -> bondsBetween u v <= 2)        -- no triple bond
    /\ exists G, closure S j1 j2 = some G /\
       exists G', Relation.ReflTransGen Y2Step G G' /\ G' ~= doubledTriangle
  Lemma 4.1 (PROVED; so "no interior cut atom" is a lemma, not a clause).  If IsTVine S j1 j2, then for every
  interior v, S - v is connected.
    Proof.
      - (Z1) moves preserve 2-connectivity.  Removing a gives G - cd plus b-d, connected because a 2-connected
        graph is 2-edge-connected.  Removing w not in {a, b}: G - w is connected and the path c-a-b-d replaces cd.
      - T is 2-connected, so cl(S) is 2-connected.
      - If v were an interior cut atom of S: every component of S - v contains an interior atom.  A component made
        only of joints would mean a subdivided loop at v, but the closure is loopless.  Paths in cl(S) - v lift to
        S - v.  So cl(S) - v would be disconnected, which is a contradiction.  QED
    CHECKED: 0 of 12522 joint placements up to 41 atoms violate it.  This agrees with REF2's "redundant".
  The (Y2)-path form needs no confluence lemma: Lemma 6.2 uses only the given path.
  Equivalent generation, for proofs: cl(S) is T, Z_k(T, l), or Z_k'(Z_k(T, l), l') (N2 Lemma 1.7, Lemma 4.2).
