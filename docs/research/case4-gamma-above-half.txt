3.1  Admissible M_0 (all of these properties are used in Section 4 or by the enumeration):
     - directed, connected, loop-free;
     - two beta-atoms (in = out = 1), the rest in = out = 2;
     - no triple bond;
     - chain ends are distinct epsilon-atoms;
     - no cut vertex.
     "No cut vertex" is equivalent to "no beta-cut possible" (Step 4):
     - A beta-cut at v disconnects, so v is a cut vertex.
     - Conversely, let v be a cut vertex of degree 4 and A a component of M_0 - v.  The degree sum of A is
       4|A_eps| + 2|A_beta| - (bonds from A to v), so A receives an even number of v's bonds: exactly 2.  Flow
       conservation (in = out everywhere) makes them opposite.  So a beta-cut along them is possible.
     - A beta-atom is never a cut vertex: one side would have odd degree sum 4|A| - 1 + 2(...).
     By CASE4 Lemma 2.3, M_0 is a block of M(Q_sub) with its joints replaced by the beta-atoms.  This is not needed
     below.
3.2  Dichotomy (1.4): Y-exceptional M_1 is T-type (|M_1| odd) or Q-type (|M_1| even), never both.
3.3  LEMMA (Q-type = vines (II), m >= 1).  If M_1 is Q-type, then M_0 is, as an undirected graph with joints b, b',
     the vine (II) with m >= 1 double bonds; m = 1 is the Q-block.
     Proof.
     - A route ending in Q is inverted by (Z) moves (L1939): M_1 = Q or M_1 = Z_n(Q, l), n >= 1.
     - A second (Z) is impossible: Z_n(Q) already has two triple bonds (the rest of the quadruple bond, and
       a_1 = b_1), and a second (Z) would add a third, while M_1 has at most two (1.4).
     - Every triple bond of M_1 must contain a new bond, and a beta-chain gives only ONE new bond.
     - If M_1 = Q: two chains on two copies of the quadruple bond (one doubly subdivided copy would leave a triple
       bond).  This is e = f, e - b - f, e - b' - f: the Q-block, i.e. (II) with m = 1.
     - If M_1 = Z_n(Q): one beta-atom on a copy of e = f, the other on a copy of a_1 = b_1.  The ladder
       (a_1=b_1), ..., (a_n=b_n), (e=f) has n + 1 double bonds, with rails a_j - a_{j+1}, b_j - b_{j+1}, a_n - e,
       b_n - f.  The joint b' is adjacent to a_1, b_1 and the joint b to e, f.  This is vine (II) with
       m = n + 1, as in the B2 template (II) with joints 0, 1.  []
     Enumeration: all 77 directed Q-type classes with |M_1| <= 13 are (II) with m = 1..6 (out_enum_classes.txt).

4. THEOREM A AND ITS PROOF
=====================================================================================================================
THEOREM A.  Let d >= 3 and 1/2 < gamma < 1.  Let eta be small depending on (d, gamma): 0 < eta << gamma - 1/2, as
DH23 L438 / L2076 and TARGET L606 already require.  Let M_0 be a case-(4) component (Sec. 1.1) whose closure M_1 is
T-type.  Then, uniformly in the parameters (c_v, beta_v, k_l^0), in particular in the gap r != 0,
      A_0 <= C (C^+)^{-4} delta^{-1} (log L)^{C} L^{-10 eta},
and (finalcount) (L1820) holds for M_0.  The same holds with "even component of M_fin" replaced by any connected
M_0 of the shape 3.1 (LG at the epsilon-atoms is not used).

PROOF.  Let S be the beta-chain removed first, and S' the other chain if there are two.  Either choice works; both
choices are checked mechanically for every class.  The operations, with Delta chi and deviation D (L1770):

Step 1 (operation (c) on S, L1861).
  - S goes from u to v, with u != v epsilon-atoms (1.1).
  - Removing it does not disconnect M_0 (parity, CASE4 Lemma 2.4(i)).  Indeed, if A is the side containing u, its
    degree sum is 4(|A_eps| - 1) + 3 + 2|A_beta|, which is odd.
  - Delta E = -2 and Delta V = -1 for one beta-atom; Delta E = -3 and Delta V = -2 for two.  In both cases
    Delta chi = -1.
  - Count: if S = u -> b -> v with bonds x, y, then y - x = c_b, and |y|^2 - |x|^2 lies in a window of length
    delta^{-1} L^{-2 gamma} (b is restricted, L680).  If S = u -> b -> b' -> v with bonds x, y, z, then y - x = c_b
    and z - y = c_{b'}.  Dropping the constraint at b' only enlarges the set, and the rest is the same.  In both
    cases the vectors of S are determined by x, so (K1) with R ~ |c_b| >= 1/L gives
        #S <~ delta^{-1} L^{(d-gamma) + gamma_0}.
  - P changes by at most (log L)^{O(1)}: only O(1) atoms of maximal ladders are touched, and each X <= (log L)^2;
    this is DH23's own remark at L1788.
  - Hence  D_1 <= #S . L^{-(d-gamma)} (C^+ delta^{-1/2})^{-1} (log L)^{C} <~ (C^+)^{-1} delta^{-1/2} (log L)^C L^{gamma_0}.
  - This step is valid for every gap.  No LG or SG is used at b.

Step 2 (operation (d) on S', if S' exists).
  - Replace S' = u' -> b' -> v' by a bond u' -> v' (L1865).  Then Delta chi = 0 and D_2 <~ (log L)^C (K4).
  - The result is exactly M_1 \ l, where l = u -> v is the bond that operation (d) would have created from S.
    (In the one-chain case Step 1 gives M_1 \ l directly.)

Step 3 ((Y)-reduction of M_1 to T, carrying the missing bond l).
  Fix a complete (Y)-route M_1 = M^(0) -> M^(1) -> ... -> M^(k) = T, made of complete (Y) sequences.
  - Every sequence ends in (Y2): a (Y1) leaves an atom of degree <= 2 forever, and T is 4-regular.
  - k <= 2: a sequence ends when no new triple bond forms, so each sequence removes one of the at most two triple
    bonds of M_1 and creates none.  (If it met the other triple bond, a quadruple bond would form; that is Q-type.)
  - Put l^(0) := l.  If l^(i) is removed by the i-th sequence, let l^(i+1) be that sequence's new bond; otherwise
    l^(i+1) := l^(i).  With n_i the number of (Y2) steps of the i-th sequence:
      LEMMA 4.1.  A(M^(i) \ l^(i)) <~ (log L)^C A(M^(i+1) \ l^(i+1)), and
                  Delta chi(M^(i) \ l^(i) -> M^(i+1) \ l^(i+1)) = -2 n_i.
      Proof.
      - If l^(i) is removed in the sequence, this is the second statement of ylem2 (L1950, proof L1956).
      - If not, repeat the proof of the first statement (L1954) with M^(i) \ l^(i) in place of M^(i).  That count
        involves only:
          * the 2n_i removed atoms and their 4n_i + 1 bonds;
          * the two exit bonds l_1, l_2, whose difference and squared-norm difference are fixed by DH21 Lemma 9.14,
            because the removed set is attached to the rest only through l_1, l_2;
          * the new bond, given the vector k_{l_2} + g(k_{l_1} - k_{l_2}) with |g| <= 3.
        None of these is l^(i), so the proof is unchanged.
      - Components: M^(i) is 4-regular and connected, so it has no bridge (every edge cut in a graph with all
        degrees even has even size).  Hence M^(i) \ l^(i) is connected, as is M^(i+1) \ l^(i+1).  So F does not
        change, and Delta chi is -2 n_i, as at L1956.  []
  - Hence  A(M_1 \ l) <~ (log L)^{C} A(T \ l*)  with l* := l^(k) a bond of T.  The ladder factors P are carried by
    (K3).

Step 4 (the gain: T minus one bond, (K2)).
  - Let l* join e and f, and let g be the third atom of T.  In T \ l*, e and f have degree 3 and are still joined by
    the other copy m of their double bond.  Each of them has two bonds to g, and g has degree 4.
  - Remove e and f with their five bonds.  What is left is the isolated atom g:
      chi(T \ l*) = 5 - 3 + 1 = 3,   chi(g) = 0 - 1 + 1 = 0,   Delta chi = -3,   F unchanged.
  - The vectors of the five bonds solve the atom systems at e and f.  These form a system {(1,2,3),(1,4,5)} as at
    L2096:
      * bond 1 = m appears at e and f with opposite signs;
      * the other four bonds appear once;
      * no tuple has three equal signs, because in, out <= 2 at e and at f.
  - By Lemma 2.1 and (K2) (gamma > 1/2 branch):
        #{five vectors} <= C delta^{-2} L^{3(d-gamma) - gamma_0 - 10 eta}.
  - P(T \ l*) <= (log L)^4, since T \ l* has two double bonds, and A(g) = 1.  Hence
        A(T \ l*) <= C delta^{-2} L^{3(d-gamma)-gamma_0-10 eta} . L^{-3(d-gamma)} (C^+ delta^{-1/2})^{-3} (log L)^4
                  =  C (C^+)^{-3} delta^{-1/2} (log L)^4 L^{-gamma_0 - 10 eta}.
  - The two orientations of T: in-degree = out-degree = 2 forces the same number a in {0,1,2} of copies e->g, g->f,
    f->e on all three sides.
      * Cyclic (a = 0 or 2): m is the odd entry at both e and f, case (i) of Lemma 2.1, zeta = x+y-v-z.
      * Anti-parallel (a = 1): m is the odd entry at neither, case (iv), zeta = x-v.
    Both orientations occur as final T (proof_steps.py: 4055 and 4711 runs).

Step 5 (total, and (finalcount)).
  - The Delta chi add up to -1 + 0 - 2(n_1 + n_2) - 3 = -chi_0: the normalisation L^{-(d-gamma) chi_0} is used
    exactly.  This is checked for every class and every choice of S.
  - Multiplying the deviations:
        A_0 <= D_1 . D_2 . (log L)^{2C} . A(T \ l*)
            <= C (C^+)^{-4} delta^{-1} (log L)^{C} L^{gamma_0} L^{-gamma_0 - 10 eta}
            =  C (C^+)^{-4} delta^{-1} (log L)^{C} L^{-10 eta}.
  - For L >= L_0(delta, eta) (L is taken large depending on delta, DH23 L438), A_0 <= L^{-9 eta}.
  - Interpolation (1.3, theta = eta^3) with (roughbd) A_0 <~ L^{-eta^2 rho_0 + 3 C_0}:
        A_0 <= L^{-9 eta (1 - eta^3)} L^{eta^3 (-eta^2 rho_0 + 3 C_0)} <= L^{-eta^2 - eta^5 rho_0},
    which is (finalcount) with V_alpha = 0, V_beta = 2, G = 1.  It holds once 9 eta - 9 eta^4 - 3 C_0 eta^3 >= eta^2.
    (roughbd) applies to M_0 whatever the gap: moleprop2's last statement allows w = 2 SG atoms, L1834.  []

       total                    -chi_0   C (C^+)^{-4} delta^{-1} (log L)^C L^{-10 eta}
     - gamma_0 = 1 - gamma here.
     - eta^2, eta^5 and rho_0 enter only through the interpolation in Step 5.
     - The large-gap budget of Lemma 2.4 is not used.
4.3  Where gamma > 1/2 is used.  ONLY in Step 4, in the branch of (countc2-1).  The inequality L^{gamma-1} <=
     L^{1-gamma} in Step 1 is only a convenience.  At gamma <= 1/2 the same steps give D_1 <~ L^{gamma} = L^{gamma_0}
     (from min(R^{-1}L^{-gamma}, L^gamma)) and A(T \ l*) <~ L^{-gamma_0}: total O(1), no gain.  This is sharp, by the
     T-bead lower bound A_0 >= c_d (C^+)^{-4} (CASE4 Lemma 4.1).  So the threshold 1/2 is exactly the branch point of
     DH23 Lemma A.2(3).
4.4  Which beta-constraint is used.  Only the one on the chain S removed in Step 1, and only through the crude bound
     (K1), which costs L^{gamma_0} for EVERY gap.  The constraint on S' is discarded by operation (d).  Hence
     small/large gap, vine or not, LG at b, b' are irrelevant.  In particular Theorem A also re-proves case (4) for
     the T-type drawn vines (III)-(VIII), without "vine => LG" (L1885) and without gamma <= 1 - 2 eta.
4.5  Equivalent formulation (a sharpened Prop 10.4(2) for gamma > 1/2).  Steps 3-4 show:
       if 1/2 < gamma < 1 and M is a 4-regular pseudomolecule with at most two triple bonds that (Y)-reduces to T,
       then A(M \ l) <~ (log L)^C L^{-gamma_0-10 eta} for EVERY bond l.
     This is the second alternative of Prop 10.4(2) (L1840), with margin 10 eta in place of eta/4.  So for gamma > 1/2
     the exceptional set of Prop 10.4(2)' (YLEM1 R2) shrinks to the Q-type graphs (Q and Z_n(Q)), and the case-(4)
     sentence L1885 can read: "in the latter case M_1 is Q-type, so M_0 is a vine (II) with m >= 1 (Lemma 3.3), hence
     LG by Prop 9.5 under (E0), and operation (c) ...".
4.6  Why CASE4 Lemma 5.1's route (Lemma A.2(1) with the second beta-constraint) is not enough.  In the anti-parallel
     T-bead, after the first chain:
       - the second beta-constraint combines with one product constraint (gain L^{-10 eta});
       - the last count is then an anti-parallel double bond, which gains only L^{-eta} from LG instead of
         L^{-gamma_0};
       - total L^{gamma_0 - 11 eta}.
     The truth there is a second moment sum_s N(s)^2 (N(s) = #{a : a.s in a window}), which is exactly (basiccount02)
     with zeta = x - v.  So the A.2(3) branch is the natural tool.  We found no order of operations that avoids it
     (orders tried: either chain first; Lemma A.2(1) at either end; the double bonds first).
4.7  Heuristic sizes (NOT used; for orientation).
     - True count of T \ l, generic parameters: about L^{3d - 4 gamma}, i.e. A ~ L^{-gamma}, far below
       L^{-gamma_0-10 eta}.
     - True normalised count of a tiny-gap T-bead: about L^{2 - 4 gamma}.
     - count_numeric.py (d = 3, delta = 1, v = 0, centres 0, max over windows) shows the ratio
       C_2 / L^{3(d-gamma)-gamma_0} with these local slopes in L in [8, 24]:
         gamma = 0.5:   +0.03 ... +0.12 (flat);
         gamma = 0.75:  -0.4 ... -0.6 (heuristic -0.5);
         gamma = 0.9:   about -0.67 (heuristic -0.8).
       This illustrates the branch of (K2); it is not evidence of uniformity over parameters.
