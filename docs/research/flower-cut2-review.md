## Residual denominator route: a rigorous partial result

DH21 frozen L2431-2444 defines q_v=alpha_v+sum_{w child of v} d_w q_w and bounds the time integral by a sum of products of <q_v>^{-1}. Thus

alpha_v=q_v-sum_{w child of v} d_w q_w.

If r distinct marked vertices have |alpha_v|>=A, each has a witness q of magnitude at least A/4 among its own vertex and its three children. A q appears in at most two witness neighborhoods: its own vertex and its parent. Hence at least ceil(r/2) DISTINCT denominators are large. This is true for every binary d assignment, without an independence assumption on resonance values. Assigning a witness independently and multiplying r gains is invalid, but bounded congestion repairs that error on the unreduced forest.

For example a two-vertex chain with alpha_child=A, alpha_parent=-A and d_child=1 has q_child=A and q_parent=0. Two marks share the single large denominator. Its time integral is (up to the pi normalization) integral_0^1 exp(-iAt) integral_0^t exp(iAu) du dt = 1/(iA)+O(A^{-2}), so a proposed M^{-2} gain is false. This does not refute the requested exponent 2/18.

After fixing the large q values, the remaining triangular denominator integration can retain logarithmic bounds: remove the fixed q coordinates and integrate all others in q coordinates, each of unit Jacobian. But the cardinality cost of each fixed q, and compatibility with the actual decoration summation, must be proved uniformly. A per-mark L^{C d} cost is affordable; L^{Cdn} per mark is not automatically affordable. The source one-mark finite-value argument does not by itself establish this uniform multivariate cardinality bound. Nor does the above charging automatically survive regular-pair identifications or eliminating intermediate nodes: the congestion must be rechecked on the actual reduced representation.

## Upper Omega cutoffs

A bounded alpha-space multiplier contracts absolute L1 and local-sup/absolute denominator norms pointwise. Thus upper cutoffs can ride in those norms whenever the reduction identity leaves their alpha arguments available. Derivative norms require Leibniz estimates; a smooth cutoff at alpha-scale B has kth derivative O(B^{-k}). Products of O(n) factors incur combinatorial/exponential-in-n costs, not intrinsically powers of M. Uniformity for small B and the allowed coefficient constants still require checking.

Fourier expansion of chi(Omega/M) inserts exp(i tau Omega/M), which shifts the node clock by tau/(pi delta L^{2gamma} M). Tolerance of exp(i lambda t) changes the frequency alpha, and is a different operation. No implication between these tolerances is automatic. Momentum-space derivatives can also see a large root momentum. The absolute alpha-space norm route avoids that derivative problem but only after a genuine coefficient/reduction identity has been established.

For the regular part DH21 L940-942 bounds an integral with max_v <alpha_v>^{1/8}, not a product over marks. It yields only one M^{-1/8} tail per coefficient. From that norm alone one cannot infer r tail gains: a smooth bump of unit volume centered at (M,...,M), with amplitude M^{-1/8}, satisfies the max-weight bound but has tail mass M^{-1/8}, whereas an r-fold M^{-1/8} estimate would require M^{-r/8}. This is a counterexample to the norm implication, not to actual regular coefficients. A proof must establish either bounded mark occupancy per independent coefficient, or a genuinely multiweighted regular-chain estimate, with the anchored-pair case included. If each factor carries at most two marks, one 1/8 gain for two marks supplies exponent 1/16 per mark, enough for the desired 1/18 per mark, leaving a small interpolation margin. This occupancy invariant is a precise promising route, not a verified fact here.

## Second refutation pass: localized spatial Fourier algebra route

The parent's proposed route survives this independent refutation attempt and resolves the specific large-root-momentum objection. Earlier discussion calling such weights momentum-space rough must not be interpreted as a Fourier-L1 obstruction.

Here is the precise local lemma. Let Q have uniformly bounded quadratic coefficients on R^D, D=O(d), let phi be a fixed smooth compactly supported unit-box cutoff, chi Schwartz, M>=1, and b,v arbitrary. Then

|| Fourier[phi(x) chi((b+v.x+Q(x))/M)] ||_1 <= C_{phi,chi,D,Q},

uniformly in b,v,M. Fourier expansion in chi gives exp(iub/M) exp(iu v.x/M) phi(x) exp(iuQ(x)/M). The middle factor merely translates spatial Fourier frequency. Sobolev control of Fourier L1 of the last two factors gives C(1+|u|/M)^s for a fixed integer s>D/2 (using sufficiently many derivatives), and integrating against |chihat(u)| is uniform. This proves the claim. It applies to a resonance in its O(d) incident momentum variables regardless of the momentum centers. Products of N such factors have Fourier-measure variation <=C^N. Affine pullback to independent conservation coordinates preserves this upper bound on Fourier-measure variation, even when the pullback map has large coefficients; one need not pay its derivative operator norm. Insert compact unit-box cutoffs before this step, as supplied by the source localization. For output spatial Fourier norms, include the localized external momentum variables as well.

TARGET frozen L1625 explicitly says the sumint proof expands W spatially and “only need[s] to prove uniform estimates” in the spatial modulation parameters. This is the appropriate interface, distinct from R58 temporal modulation. Thus this lemma supplies the required upper-cutoff insertion at the localized sumint level, with C^N rather than root-dependent or M-dependent loss.

The core-vine cancellation is also favorable: frozen TARGET L1883-1911 forms replaceterm1 before the momentum sum is estimated; the leading term at a common x_* is “exactly zero after summing over all (Q,T) in B.” This cancellation is pointwise in x_* (and the remaining internal bond momenta), after the coherent approximants have been summed. The vine-top resonance cutoff has precisely that character: its skeleton momenta are common under the decoration bijection. It can remain outside the coherent cancellation, then enter the spatial Fourier expansion. Differences in cases (a)-(c) need only expand the unweighted approximants; the common weight need not be differentiated in the x_*-y_* comparison. ## Third pass: marked-node excision and BV integration

Bounded verdict: plausible and not refuted, but it is a new multi-stub estimate rather than an already established consequence of the single-stub design C ruling. One free mark per original tree is favorable: before any reductions, original clocks in distinct trees are distinct. After removing the marked oscillatory phase, variation of its clock affects only the parent inequality, up to three branching-child inequalities, and interval endpoints. A mixed first derivative in all r=2P marked clocks therefore has at most 4^r internal-face terms, with O(r) additional pins; no cross-tree clock identification is present in the original time domain. Endpoint faces at the layer interval boundaries, and faces against the original fixed flower-parent time s, must also be included. Full multivariate BV integration by parts involves lower-dimensional faces and lower mixed derivatives as well as the full mixed derivative. A product BV norm can package these with C^r terms. Equal-time faces are limits of strict time inequalities, and need a consistent trace convention.

The uniform multi-stub weighted garden theorem is the essential unproved gate: it must tolerate O(P) arbitrary fixed clocks, their adjacent fixed-fixed edges, and fixed local momentum labels, with loss L^{C(d)P} independent of total tree order N except the already allowed exponential-in-N combinatorics. It must retain the required delta^{2nu N} exponent. A single-stub theorem does not imply this by iteration unless its hypotheses and constants remain uniform after each insertion.

The delta bookkeeping needs explicit treatment. The oscillatory denominator is (delta L^{2gamma} M)^{-r}. If one excises r nodes, retains their original delta prefactors, and bounds the residual order N-r at delta^{2nu(N-r)}, the resulting product is proportional to delta^{2nu N-2nu r} L^{-2gamma r} M^{-r}, apart from spatial normalization factors. Thus simply asserting delta^{2nu N} with a polynomial L loss silently discards delta^{-2nu r}. A relation between L and delta, permissible constant dependence, or stronger residual delta slack can repair this; it must be stated. Fractionally weakening the M exponent alone does not automatically remove the delta loss.

Local mode fixing is plausible after unit localization: each incident momentum is a signed sum of at most O(N) unit-box leaf variations, so the number of lattice labels in its corresponding O(N)-sized box is (NL)^{O(d)}. For O(P) pins this is (NL)^{O(d)P}; under N<=L^C it fits the budget. But one must not condition on independent full original leaf-center assignments if doing so destroys coherent LF family summation. A valid proof localizes by a twist-compatible partition or sums the family before localization, and only fixes the O(P) special labels. Merely bounding every fixed shape separately can lose precisely the LF cancellation that supplies the garden gain. The source already treats O(P) exceptional vines touching special nodes at polynomial L cost, which is a promising way to isolate these new pins, but it is not the full multi-stub proof.

Accordingly this route is a concrete mathematical work package, not circular if its multi-stub theorem is proved directly by the source reductions. It is circular if that theorem is postulated with the desired high-M gain or justified only by saying design C is stable. No actual counterexample to the resulting theorem is established here.
